# Amortised credit head: build plan

**Status:** design. Gate 0 passed (`docs/dam_result.md`, split-half reliability 0.89).
**Supersedes:** the rate-space DAM arm, which is recorded as a negative in
[`dam_result.md`](dam_result.md). This keeps DAM's IDEA -- per-coordinate credit
assignment -- and discards its estimator.
**Prior art:** Discrete Guidance Matching, [arXiv:2509.21912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21912)
(ICLR 2026), local copy at repo root. It trains a guidance network to estimate a
conditional expectation by minimising a Bregman divergence, giving guidance that needs
"only a single forward pass in each sampling step". That is the mechanism below.

## 1. Why this and not more DAM

DAM estimates a per-coordinate value DIFFERENCE by Monte Carlo, freshly at every state,
from K=8-12 continuations. Measured reliability of one such instruction: **0.144**. The
same instruction measured with 384 continuations: **0.890**. The signal is real and
strongly reproducible; the estimator cannot see it.

A regressor does not pay per instruction. Every coordinate of every sample at every
state supervises one shared function, which is the pooling gain the diagnosis says is
missing. DAM's own related work names the choice it made:

> "Rather than estimating the value function directly, our DAM utilizes an estimator for
> the exponential value difference."

We estimate the level, directly.

## 2. The object

DAM's tilted path measure gives a tilted posterior over endpoints:

    p*(x1 | xt)  =  p_base(x1 | xt) * exp(-g(x1)) / Z

Marginalise to one coordinate -- which is all DeFoG's rate ever reads:

    p*(x1^i = c | xt)  ~  p_base(x1^i = c | xt) * m_t(xt)[i, c]

    where   m_t(xt)[i, c]  =  E_base[ exp(-g(x1)) | x1^i = c, xt ]

**`m` is the whole build.** It is a positive tensor with exactly the shape of DeFoG's
clean-graph head -- `(bs, n, dx)` and `(bs, n, n, de)` -- and the steered head is an
elementwise product followed by renormalisation.

Note it is `E[exp(-g)]`, NOT `exp(E[-g])`. Regressing the reward and exponentiating
afterwards is a different, Jensen-biased object. This is the same trap that `n_z`
averaging had to avoid.

## 3. The loss

Use the generalised KL already in `defog/core/dam.py`:

    L = sum_i  gkl( m_theta(xt, t)[i, x1^i],  exp(-g(x1)) )        masked to real coords

`gkl(u, w) = u - w + w log(w/u)` is the Bregman divergence generated by `F = <x, log x>`
-- exactly DGM's choice -- and **its minimiser in `u` is the conditional MEAN of `w`**,
which is the definition of `m`. Squared error would also have the right minimiser, but
the target `exp(-g)` is positive and heavy-tailed and gKL handles that far better.

Each `(xt, x1)` pair supervises one entry per coordinate: the class `x1` actually took.
Other classes at that coordinate are supervised by other samples from the same `xt`.
This is standard conditional-expectation regression and is well posed.

**Why this is not the scoring head that failed.** That one labelled a broken intermediate
graph with the logP of the ANCESTOR it was corrupted from -- the same graph reached from
two ancestors got two labels, so it was not a function of its input, and it was mixed
with own-logP labels on valid draws, i.e. two different conditional distributions in one
dataset. Here the input is `(xt, t, i, c)`, the label is the reward of a genuine endpoint
drawn from `xt` whose coordinate `i` equals `c`, and the minimiser is a well-defined
conditional expectation. Label noise is the point of regression, not a defect.

## 4. What it reuses

| piece | where | status |
|---|---|---|
| `gkl` | `defog/core/dam.py` | audited, tested |
| `rate_basis`, `marginal_rate` | `defog/core/dam.py` | audited; the reusable result of the DAM work |
| `renoise_states`, `draw_times` | `defog/core/renoise.py` | verified against the kernel, atol=0 |
| rollout + reward plumbing | `defog/core/rl.py` | GDPO's, in production |
| head shape, masking, edge symmetry | `defog/core/model.py` | unchanged |

The steered rate is `marginal_rate(normalise(p_base * m), BX, BE)`. Because it modifies
the HEAD and the rate is rebuilt from it by the sampler, **the correction transfers
across `eta` and `omega` automatically** -- which directly sidesteps the eta=1-vs-25
mismatch that is a live defect of any rate-space method (`dam_result.md`).

## 5. Two ways to use it, in order

1. **Guidance at sampling time.** Train `m`, then multiply the head during generation.
   No adapter training at all. This is DGM's own use, it is the cheapest path to an
   outcome number, and it is where validation should start.
2. **Distil into an adapter.** Only if a deployable artefact without the extra forward
   is wanted. Train an adapter to match the guided head. Strictly later.

## 6. Gates

Each gate has a pass criterion fixed BEFORE it runs, and a negative control. No gate is
judged on `resid`, which failed three independent ways and is retired.

**Gate 0 -- is there anything to learn.** PASSED. Split-half reliability 0.890, and
`resid-2` (per-state per-class mean removed) 0.802 vs `raw` 0.816, so the signal is
coordinate-specific rather than a per-element preference.

**Gate 1 -- does the head fit better than the trivial baselines.** On HELD-OUT states,
gKL of the fitted `m` must beat (a) a global constant and (b) a per-class scalar, and
beat them by a margin larger than the seed-to-seed spread. *This is exactly where the
earlier scoring head died -- it landed worse than predicting the mean -- so it is the
gate, not a formality.* Cost: hours-GPU.

**Gate 2 -- does it recover the instruction we already measured.** Take the existing
K=384 pool. Compute the empirical per-coordinate shift, and the shift the trained `m`
implies. Correlate, residualised at `resid-2`. Pass: correlation >= 0.6, against a
ceiling of 0.89 set by the data's own reliability (nothing can exceed it). Cost:
minutes-GPU. **This gate cannot be passed by a per-class scalar**, which is what makes
it worth running.

**Gate 3 -- does it move a molecule.** Guided sampling vs (a) base+adapter unguided and
(b) GDPO, on logP MAE against the conditioning target. Paired, >= 3 seeds, >= 256
molecules per arm, validity reported alongside. Baseline already measured: **MAE 0.6730,
54/64 decode**. Pass: MAE improvement outside the seed spread, with validity not
degraded. Cost: hours-GPU.

**Gate 4 -- how much of the available gain was captured.** Reweighting the base's own
samples is worth **+0.97 reward** at lambda=1 (`scripts/marginal.py`). Report guided
sampling's gain as a fraction of that ceiling. This is a number, not a pass/fail, and it
is what the write-up needs.

## 7. What kills it

* **Gate 1 fails.** The high-reward region is rare under base sampling, so `m` is fitted
  worst exactly where it matters. Mitigation is a positive-sample buffer with re-noising
  (CDM's fix); if that is also needed, cost rises to multi-day.
* **Gate 2 passes but Gate 3 does not.** The instruction is learnable and correct and
  still does not move molecules -- which would mean the per-coordinate marginal channel
  is genuinely too narrow, and the honest conclusion is to use GDPO.
* **lambda has no useful setting.** It is a free knob at inference here, so this is
  cheap to explore, but a head that only helps at a lambda that wrecks validity is not a
  result.

## 8. Cost

Head training hours-GPU; Gates 1-2 within a day; Gate 3 hours-GPU. Total roughly a day
of build plus a day of compute, versus the multi-day rate-space arm it replaces.

## 9. What gets written up either way

If Gate 3 passes: per-coordinate credit works on a non-masked graph CTMC once the
estimator is amortised rather than sampled -- on a problem the DAM authors name as open
("Applying DAM to non-masked CTMC's presents an interesting future work", p.11).

If Gate 3 fails: a boundary result, with the reliability curve (0.144 at K=8, 0.890 at
K=384) and the coherence contrast (`oxy-max` 1.000 vs `logp-match` 0.577 at matched ESS)
as a reusable predictor of when per-coordinate credit is worth buying.
